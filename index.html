Who gets into cancer trials depends on the doctor and the practice

Of 205,743 patients with advanced cancer in 107 community practices, 5.2% joined a trial. About a quarter of physicians accounted for 84% of enrollments, and nearly 30% enrolled no one. Published with an invited commentary.

LinkedInOctober 2026

Why precision oncology trials fill so slowly

Phase III trials that require a common biomarker enroll about 0.2 patients per site per month, compared with 1.3 for other trials. Most of the delay sits in steps no protocol counts: ordering the test, finding tissue, and waiting for the result.

Short bio

Third person, free to reuse for introductions and programs.

Changchuan (Charles) Jiang, MD, MPH, is a medical oncologist and health services researcher in Dallas, Texas. He treats prostate, bladder, and kidney cancers and holds a joint faculty appointment in medicine and public health. His work focuses on access to cancer care, including clinical trial enrollment, supportive care, insurance and financial barriers, and clinical workflows and AI. He leads a prostate cancer trial access program funded by the American Cancer Society and co-chairs the Health Access and Disparities Committee of the Multinational Association of Supportive Care in Cancer (MASCC). He has published more than 80 peer-reviewed papers, in journals including JAMA, JAMA Network Open, Nature Reviews Clinical Oncology, JNCI, and Annals of Internal Medicine. He trained at Soochow University, Yale School of Public Health, Mount Sinai, and Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center.

Work

What I work on

Most of my projects fall into one of these four areas.

1

Clinical trial access

About 1 in 20 patients with advanced cancer in community oncology joins a therapeutic trial, and our national study found that the physician and practice a patient happens to see matter more than anything measured about the patient. That finding shapes most of my trial work. I lead a prostate cancer trial access program at a county safety-net hospital and community sites, funded by the American Cancer Society, and direct GU trial optimization for the safety-net program. Patient navigation and physician engagement there have doubled enrollment of underrepresented patients. An ASCO Career Development Award supports the national work on physician referral.

Subspecialized physicians, physicians who had enrolled patients before, and research-engaged practices all had much higher odds of enrolling patients.
JAMA Netw Open 2026Nat Rev Clin Oncol 2025
2

Supportive care

I co-chair the Health Access and Disparities Committee of MASCC, the Multinational Association of Supportive Care in Cancer. My research here covers transportation barriers, financial hardship, palliative and end-of-life care, and insurance hurdles for basic supportive drugs such as antiemetics. With MASCC colleagues I have also surveyed clinicians in many countries about gaps in supportive care.

Adults with a cancer history and transportation barriers had more than twice the mortality risk of adults with neither.
JNCI 2023Ann Intern Med 2024JNCCN 2026
3

Insurance and financial barriers

I study how Medicare Advantage and Part D plan design shapes cancer care, including oncologist networks, prior authorization, drug coverage, palliative care, and time to treatment. I also study how medical debt relates to when cancer is found. The work links plan benefit files, formularies, claims, cancer registries, and clinical data.

Counties with more medical debt had more late-stage diagnoses across seven cancer types.
JAMA Netw Open 2026JCO Oncol Pract 2026JAMA Netw Open 2026
4

Clinical workflow and AI

With NCCN support, I am building reflex biomarker testing and a results dashboard into the EHR for patients with metastatic prostate cancer at a safety-net program, so that a positive result leads to treatment. I serve on my cancer center's AI steering committee and write about how clinical AI should be evaluated and regulated. My own AI work is practical: language models that draft plain-language trial summaries for patients, and testing tools inside real clinic workflows.

A scoping review of language models across the oncology trial lifecycle is under review.
