Changchuan (Charles) Jiang, MD, MPH, is a medical oncologist and health services researcher in Dallas, Texas. He treats prostate, bladder, and kidney cancers and holds a joint faculty appointment in medicine and public health. His work focuses on access to cancer care, including clinical trial enrollment, supportive care, insurance benefit design, and clinical workflows and AI. He leads a prostate cancer trial access program funded by the American Cancer Society and co-chairs the Health Access and Disparities Committee of the Multinational Association of Supportive Care in Cancer (MASCC). He has published more than 80 peer-reviewed papers, in journals including Nature Reviews Clinical Oncology, JNCI, JAMA Oncology, and Annals of Internal Medicine. He trained at Soochow University, Yale School of Public Health, Mount Sinai, and Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center.

Work

What I work on

Most of my projects fall into one of these four areas.

Clinical trial access

In community oncology, about 1 in 20 patients with advanced cancer enrolls in a therapeutic trial, and the physician and practice they see explain more of that variation than the patients themselves. I lead a prostate cancer trial access program at a county safety-net hospital and community sites, funded by the American Cancer Society, and direct GU trial optimization for the safety-net program. Navigation and physician engagement have doubled enrollment of underrepresented patients there. An ASCO Career Development Award supports my national work on physician referral patterns.

National data: 205,743 patients, 1,664 physicians, 107 practices. About 20% of physicians account for 80% of enrollments. Manuscripts under review.Empowering oncologists, Nat Rev Clin Oncol 2025

Supportive care

I co-chair the Health Access and Disparities Committee of MASCC, the Multinational Association of Supportive Care in Cancer. My research in this area covers transportation barriers, financial hardship, the value of palliative care, and insurance hurdles for basic supportive drugs such as antiemetics.

Adults with a cancer history and transportation barriers had more than twice the mortality risk of adults with neither.Transportation and mortality, JNCI 2023Value of palliative care, Ann Intern Med 2024

Insurance and access

I study how Medicare Advantage and Part D plan design shapes cancer care, including oncologist networks, prior authorization, drug coverage, and time to treatment. The work links plan benefit files, formularies, claims, and clinical data.

Hormonal therapy timing under Medicare Advantage, JCO Oncol Pract 2026Oncologist networks in Medicare Advantage, JAMA Netw Open 2026

Clinical workflow and AI

With NCCN support, I am building reflex biomarker testing and a results dashboard into the EHR for patients with metastatic prostate cancer at a safety-net program, so that a positive result leads to treatment. I also serve on my cancer center's AI steering committee. My AI work is practical: large language models that draft plain-language trial summaries for patients, and evaluation of tools inside real clinical workflows.

A scoping review of large language models in oncology trials and two commentaries on clinical AI governance are under review.
